KT's steer: the writing side is where Claude is weakest; use Firecrawl to research optimal copy for anything, and do not ask KT to judge wording. This record shows the evidence and what changed. Evidence labels: [Established] read first-hand on the page named; [Reported] a source says so, not independently verified; [Inference] reasoned.

**Patterns [Established]:** superlatives ("#1"), big numbers, outcome promises ("get hired", "land it"), and, at FlexJobs, the scam fear named outright. **Gap:** none of them leads with *who can apply*, the thing our audience says wastes their time (research file, Pain 1). That is our distinct, true angle.

## 2. What the evidence says about writing it
- **Clarity over cleverness; benefit first; a supporting line that says how.** Several practitioner sources agree ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/landing-page-examples/high-converting-landing-pages/): "clarity leads to conversions"; [Linear Design](https://lineardesign.com/blog/landing-page-design-best-practices/): benefit-led headlines, with a sub-headline that adds "credibility and specificity"). [Reported], vendor blogs; the principle is consistent across them, the lift sizes are not reliable.
- **First-person button text.** ContentVerve's test, cited by [Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-rate-optimization/cta-buttons-that-convert/): "Start my free 30-day trial" beat "Start your 30-day free trial" by 90% in click-through. [Reported], one test on one site, repeated by many writers; treat the direction as plausible, the size as unknown.
- **Short reassurance next to the action** ("No credit card required"), but only if it is literally true ([Growth Atlas](https://growthatlas.eu/blogs/news/cta-microcopy-that-converts) makes the same point). [Reported].
- **Name the audience's fear and answer it with something checkable** [Inference from the research file plus FlexJobs's choice]. We cannot promise "no scams" the way FlexJobs does; we can say what we do: never charge to apply, drop postings that ask applicants to pay, show who can apply.
- **Honest limit:** the board gets roughly 50 home views a week, so an A/B test cannot reach significance for months. These choices rest on research and on the audience's own words, not on a test of ours.

## 3. What changed
| Where | Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home hero line | "...(Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby). See who can apply, then apply on the original posting." | "Fresh postings from companies' own hiring pages (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby), each showing who can apply before you spend an hour on it." | Keeps the accurate sourcing; replaces the how-to-use sentence with the benefit in the audience's own terms (time wasted: "wasted my time applying", "finding out halfway down that it's US only"). |
| "Get a free CV check" buttons (home, services) | "Get a free CV check" | "Get my free CV check" | First-person direction; the label also states the outcome. |
| Free-check submit button | "Request my free check" | "Get my free CV check" | "Request" is form language; this names what they get. |
| Free-check lead | "Send us your CV and we'll reply with the three things we'd fix first. It's free, one per person..." | "Not sure if it's your CV or the jobs? Send us your CV and we'll reply with the three things we'd fix first. It's free, one per person, and we never ask you to pay to apply..." | Opens on the doubt the audience voices ("might be that your resume sucks" vs "ghost jobs"); adds the trust line next to the ask (research file, Fear 2). All facts already true (trust rule 1). |
| Free-check "Last step" box | no handling statement | adds: "We use your CV only for this check and delete it within 30 days if you don't order." | Answers "the person asking for my CV is the scammer" at the moment of the request; matches the privacy page. |

**Kept on purpose:** the H1 "Remote jobs that show who can apply" (clear, specific, and unclaimed by competitors); "Get Job-Ready" in the header (KT's preference); the services hero "Stop applying in silence..." (uses the audience's own word).
**Not claimed:** "no scams", "#1", "real jobs", "hand-screened" (none is substantiated for us). See `2026-11-01-source-and-link-wording.md` for why overall impression matters.
