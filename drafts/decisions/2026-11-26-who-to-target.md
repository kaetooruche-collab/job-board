## Assessment
1. **For a US job seeker, we are not different.** About two-thirds of remote jobs are already open to them everywhere: LinkedIn, Indeed, We Work Remotely, Himalayas. Our main feature (where you can apply from) answers a question they rarely have. Competing for US attention means competing with the largest job sites on their home ground. (Inference)
2. **For people outside the US, the problem is real and painful**, and it is the one our product is built around. "Outside the US" is not only Africa and Asia: Canada, the UK, Europe and Latin America have the same problem and **much better supply on our board today** (64-193 jobs, against 33 for West and East Africa). (Inference from the measured numbers)
3. **KT's worry is right for low-supply regions.** A visitor from Lagos who finds 33 jobs, mostly tech and sales, may not return. That isn't an argument for the US; it's an argument for (a) not promising more than the board has, and (b) growing supply for those regions. The market has about 26,000 postings open to the Global South; we list a sliver because we track 363 employers. Alion found 4,501 employers who hire in the Global South. That is the most valuable growth lever: add those employers' boards. (Inference)
4. **Money:**
   - **Display ads** pay far more for US traffic, but ads need tens of thousands of monthly visits before they pay anything. At about 100 views a week, the geography of ad revenue does not matter yet.
   - **The CV service** ($29-149) is cheap by US standards, where resume writing commonly costs hundreds of dollars and has many competitors. It is meaningful but affordable outside the US, where a remote job paid in dollars is worth a lot. (Hypothesis; the 16 Nov demand test decides it.)
   - **Monetisation that fits a global, non-US audience:** affiliate partnerships for being paid from abroad (Wise, Payoneer, Deel and the like: hypothesis, terms not checked), and later, employers who hire globally paying to reach this audience (employer listings; a gate already in CLAUDE.md).

## Recommendation
- **Positioning:** "Remote jobs you can actually apply for, wherever you live." The niche is a problem, not a region: **people outside the US** who are tired of "US only". This is still a niche, not generic fishing; it just isn't drawn by continent.
- **Don't exclude anyone.** The site stays open to everyone, and US visitors are welcome and will find plenty. But don't spend marketing effort chasing them yet.
- **Paid targeting, if any (later):** start where supply is strong (UK, Canada, Europe, India, Latin America). Add Africa as supply grows. Let visitor-country and return-rate data decide after 4 weeks.
- **Supply work (job-board session, KT's yes needed):**
  1. Check whether Alion's open data names the employers who hire in the Global South (CC BY 4.0 allows reuse with credit).
  2. Add those that use the hiring systems we already read.
  3. Track "jobs open to the median non-US country" weekly as the board's key growth number.
- **Measure:** visitor countries (PR #80), and return visits by country, from the next weekly snapshot.
