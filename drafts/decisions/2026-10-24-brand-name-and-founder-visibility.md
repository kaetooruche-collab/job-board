# Decision review: one brand name for the job board and the career services, and whether a founder's name and face appear

Status: OPEN, needs KT's decision. Requested by KT on 23 Oct 2026. Supersedes `2026-10-23-services-brand-name.md` (which compared keeping "Kaeto Career Services" with a board-linked name). KT's direction: one polished, professional name NOT based on KT's name, used for both the board and the career services; no founder photo or name on the brand.

All screening below was run on 23-24 Oct 2026. Tools used: Vercel domain registry lookups (availability and price, read-only), the US Patent and Trademark Office public search service (live marks only), general web searches (US-only, weak), public registry records for a few domains. NOT run, because the databases block automation: EUIPO/TMview, UK IPO, WIPO Global Brand Database, Cyprus and Turkish registries, social handle checks, company registries. See the checklist `drafts/brand-name-checklist.md`.

## Part 1. Founder name and face: pros and cons (Claude's independent view)

What is on the site today (`services.html`, section "Who's behind this?"): a photo (`kt-photo.webp`), the role line "Founder, Kaeto Career Services", the claims "I've reviewed hundreds of applications..." and "built to get you noticed", and a WhatsApp button "Message Kaeto". The first name also appears in the page title, privacy notice, emails and WhatsApp templates.

KT's belief: "other sites doing similar things don't have that". Checked: it is mostly NOT true for job boards, but it is true for the big CV-builder brands.
- Job boards with named founders: Jobicy (founder named, with a quote, on its About page), Himalayas (three named founders), Working Nomads (two named founders), FlexJobs and Remote.co (founder Sara Sutton Fell, widely profiled), Remote OK (creator Pieter Levels, from one source). We Work Remotely: no founder found.
- CV services: Novoresume names its CEO and co-founder; Resume.io and TopResume present the company and "team" with no personal founder on the pages checked (several sites blocked automated reading, so this is a sample, not a survey).
- Where founders appear: nearly always on an About page, not as a headshot beside the pricing buttons of a sales page.

| | Keep a named face | Remove it, present the company |
|---|---|---|
| Trust | A real person lowers "is this a scam?" fear, common in job-seeker markets. Strongest for 1:1 services sold by chat. | Trust must come from other proof: legal entity named, process, refund promise, verification rules, real testimonials, a reachable contact. None is a face, but all are checkable. |
| Bias (KT's concern) | Real: buyers judge photo, name, apparent age, gender, accent, location. Can help or hurt, and the person cannot choose which buyers see which. | Removes that variable. Does not remove the human in WhatsApp chats; first replies will still come from a person. |
| Safety and liability | Photo plus name plus phone number makes impersonation, harassment and doxxing easier. A limited company limits legal liability for the business; it does not limit personal reputation risk. Dissatisfied buyers target a person. | Smaller personal exposure. The company still must disclose who it is (see below). |
| Brand and exit | Brand is tied to one person: hard to sell, hire writers, or stop being the face. | Easier to scale, hand over or sell. |
| Search and credibility | Google's quality guidance rewards visible expertise and who is responsible for a page. A named author helps for advice content. | Can be met with company details and sourced claims. |
| Legal | None needed for the face. | The service provider must still be identifiable: legal name, address, contact details and any registration number are required by e-commerce and consumer rules in the EU and UK and in many other places (exact duties depend on where the company and its buyers are; to be confirmed with the accountant or a lawyer). The privacy page already names KAYZ Global Ltd. |
| Unverified claims | "I've reviewed hundreds of applications" cannot be verified from the site and sits beside a promise-like line ("built to get you noticed"). KT confirmed the first on 17 Oct; the second reads as an implied outcome. | Gone with the section. |

Recommendation: remove the photo, the first name and the "Founder" role line from the brand and the sales pages, and replace the block with a company "How we work" trust block (legal entity, how an order works, who answers on WhatsApp described as "the team", refund promise already approved, link to the verification rules). Keep the legal entity wording on the privacy page. Re-check with real data later: once there are real testimonials, a named customer-facing person can be re-added without a face if wanted. Reasons to accept KT's view: bias and safety risks are real, a company brand is easier to sell, and the sales page is not where competitors show founders.
Two cautions: (1) first WhatsApp replies and emails are currently signed "Kaeto"; a company brand needs a consistent sender ("the [Brand] team") and KT should decide how to answer "who am I talking to?"; (2) the photo stays in the repository's history after deletion. If the repository is public, anyone can still see it; check that the repository is private.

## Part 2. The name

### Constraints applied (from CLAUDE.md and KT)
One name for board and services; not KT's name; no country or city (CLAUDE.md); professional and polished; works for stressed job seekers worldwide; a trust story ("every listing verified"); easy to say and spell; short enough for a WhatsApp business name and a bank-statement descriptor.

### What the domain search showed (`.com` first because email deliverability and trust)
- Of about 400 candidate names tried (compounds, meaningful words, invented words), essentially every natural `.com` is already registered. The free `.com` names are unpronounceable strings ("oraari", "nexoux"). Meaningful words all have an owner: some of them long-held (farfield.com since 1996, farreach.com since 2000, plumbline.com since 1999).
- Of the 72 domains from the pasted list of names, 60 `.com` were taken; the only free `.com` were `openroutecareers.com` and `nextstopcareers.com`. Of the 62 `.com` names tried from that list, 3 have operating businesses with that name in the same space, and 2 are advertised for sale (workora.com, upstride.com; price unknown).
- Practical rule for founders in 2026: accept `<name>jobs.com`, `get<name>.com`, `<name>.work` or `.careers` as the working domain, or buy an aftermarket name.
- The placeholder "TrueRemote": `trueremote.com` and `.io` are registered by someone else, and there is an existing remote job board called **Truly Remote** (and a spammy "Webtrulyremote"), so the name is a confusion risk in the same category. `trueremote.co` ($29.99) and `.jobs` ($159) were free, but this alone is a reason to rename.
- "Kaeto": no US live trademarks, no web business found in CV services; it sounds like "keto".

### The pasted list of names (from another assistant, never verified by it)
Screened the same way (about 60 names): 
- Domains: 60 of 62 `.com` taken.
- Trademarks: conflicts in job or career classes for CareerPoint (CareerPoint USA), CareerCraft (two live marks), CareerFoundry (CareerFoundry GmbH), CareerBoost, NextRole, Workpath (Workpath GmbH), Worknest (Worknest Limited, classes 9/35/41/42/45).
- Real businesses on the `.com`: Talentry (a recruitment platform, now part of CleverConnect), CareerBridge (work-based learning), Career Ova (a careers site), CareerCraft (Texas school programmes), Workloom (AI automation), PathWise (life-science compliance), Workpath (enterprise software).
- No operating site but the `.com` is held: Jobvera, Workzen, TalentRoute, HireReady, JobLaunch, OfferReady, CareerElevate, CareerProof, RoleReady, WorkAtlas, NextRole (purchase would need an owner's price, unknown).
- Structural weakness: most are descriptive words stuck together ("HireReady", "CareerProof", "NextRole"). Descriptive marks are the hardest to register and defend, and are hard to stand out in search. Only a few are suggestive (Upstride, Pathwise, Launchwell, Rolewise), and those either have real businesses or `.com` owners.
- Conclusion: none of the pasted names beats the screened shortlist; Jobvera.co, Careerova.co, Employra.co and Vocationa.co were free as `.co` ($29.99) but have ordinary descriptive meanings and (for Careerova) an existing careers site.
- Its closing question ("job seekers, services, or both?") is answered by our brief: both, one brand; the board brings the traffic and the services bring the revenue.

### Recommendation
Choose **Farhaven** as the single name, with **Farreach** as the fallback if a trademark attorney or a native-speaker check finds a problem. Brand lines: "Farhaven Jobs" (the board) and "Farhaven Careers" (CV, cover letter and LinkedIn services), one name, no founder.
Why not the most "obvious" candidates: the only free `.com` options are clumsy; every strong real word has a long-time owner; Farfield and Plumbline would share search results with real incumbents.
Honest limits of this pick: the name is chosen on clearance and story, not tested on customers; one US applicant (a games company) holds an allowed FARHAVEN mark in unrelated classes; the exact `.com` is not ours; "haven" can also suggest "tax haven" to some ears; EU, UK, Turkey, Cyprus and international trademark and company searches are not done.

### Suggested domain plan if KT approves (cost about $50 for the first year; NOT bought)
- Primary web and email domain: `farhavenjobs.com` is descriptive for the board, but the brand covers services too; a neutral `.com` such as `getfarhaven.com` or `farhavenhq.com` serves email and the whole brand. Recommended: register `farhavenhq.com` (email and primary) and `farhaven.work` (the board's natural vanity address), plus `farhavenjobs.com` and `farhavencareers.com` as redirects. About $45 in the first year at $11.25 each.
- Defensive: `farhaven.org` ($9.99) and `.io` ($14.99, renews $46) only if desired.
- Keep trueremote.jobs@gmail.com working until the new domain's email is authenticated and tested; announce the change.

5. KT's approval before any purchase or rename.
