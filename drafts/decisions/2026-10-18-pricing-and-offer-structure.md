# Decision review: services, pricing and offer structure

Status: PROPOSED (18 Oct 2026). Nothing here is published. Prices, package names, revision limits, the 12-post commitment, the 30-day period and the 500+ target change only with KT's explicit approval (CLAUDE.md).

## 0. The answer in five lines
1. Price is not the biggest lever. Orders per month are (traffic x enquiry rate x close rate): the same business can sell 1 or 45 orders a month on the same prices (table in section 5). Fix the path to a WhatsApp enquiry first, price second.
2. The current ladder is right at the bottom and wrong at the top. Essential $29 and Professional $49 are sensible entry prices for a new seller with no reviews. Executive $99 is the problem: about 30 hours of work (54 if the client starts near 24 connections) for $99 is roughly $3 an hour after fees.
3. Recommended ladder: Essential $29, Professional $49 (labelled "Recommended"), Executive $149 with a bounded scope (about 18 hours), plus a monthly "LinkedIn Growth" continuation at $99 a month and a short add-on menu. Same three-tier layout, one clear recommended middle tier.
4. Expected effect, from my assumptions (not measured): average order value up about 35-40 percent and the hourly yield after fees up from about $5-6 to about $7 an hour, with fewer hours on the heaviest customers.
5. Add the things that create orders: a "get a CV for this job" button on each job page that opens WhatsApp, a free quick CV check to start conversations, and the first real testimonials. These need no price change.

## 4. Recommended structure
### 4.1 Core ladder (three options, as the research on plan pages recommends: no more than three to four)
| Tier | Price | Scope | Role in the ladder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Essential | $29 | CV only, up to 2 revisions, 5 working days (unchanged) | entry |
| Professional | $49 | Essential + cover letter + LinkedIn profile setup, up to 3 revisions (unchanged); labelled "Recommended for most job seekers" | the tier most buyers should pick |
| Executive | $149 | Professional + branding strategy + 12 LinkedIn posts over 30 days + manual targeted connection building at a fixed weekly volume for 4 weeks, with an aim of moving towards 500+ total connections (or 100 new relevant connections if already above 500). No open-ended continuation. | premium and anchor |

Why $149 and not $199: at about 18 hours, $149 gives about $7.4 an hour net, which matches the other tiers; $199 gives about $9.9 an hour but is the riskier jump for a buyer base with unknown purchasing power. A $50 step above the UK CV-only tier of GBP 149 is fine because the package contains much more than a CV. If the first Executive buyers come from higher-income countries without hesitation, move to $199.

Do not label Professional "Most popular" or "Most chosen" until that is true. "Recommended" is an honest recommendation, not a statistic.

### 4.2 Recurring product: LinkedIn Growth, $99 a month
12 posts a month plus weekly targeted connection requests (within LinkedIn's limits). Sold to Executive clients at day 30 and to Professional clients who want the LinkedIn part. It replaces "we continue until you reach 500+" with a paid, optional continuation, so the heavy work is paid for. Month to month, cancel any time before the next month starts. Paid manually each month by the same WhatsApp arrangement, so no automatic charging and no payment processor.

### 4.3 Add-ons (offered in the WhatsApp message after the package is chosen; these are the "order bump")
| Add-on | Price | Hours (est.) |
|---|---|---|
| Cover letter for an Essential order | $15 | 1 |
| LinkedIn profile setup for an Essential order | $25 | 2 |
| Extra revision round | $10 | 1 |
| Second CV for a different target role | $15 | 1.5 |
| 48-hour priority delivery | $15 | 0 (queue priority) |
| Interview preparation sheet (likely questions and answer outlines for one target role) | $29 | 2 |
Only offer the ones KT is willing to deliver at that price. Add-ons raise average order value without adding a fourth plan column.

### 4.4 Not now
- A fourth, application-only "Concierge" tier from $499. Keep it as a sentence ("Need a longer programme? Ask about a custom plan") and build it only after real requests. Reason: the decoy effect is weak in realistic settings (Frederick, Lee and Baskin replication, earlier review) and a tier nobody can deliver at scale adds nothing.
- Local-currency price lists. Complexity is high; learn buyer countries first.
- Discount codes and fake scarcity. Anything like "only 3 spots left" must be true or it breaks the site's own trust standard.

## 5. Where the real money is: volume
Orders per month = visitors x enquiry rate x close rate (all three are assumptions; I have no data yet).
| Visitors a month | enquiry 1% / close 20% | enquiry 2% / close 25% | enquiry 3% / close 30% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 500 | 1 | 2.5 | 4.5 |
| 2,000 | 4 | 10 | 18 |
| 5,000 | 10 | 25 | 45 |
The spread between the corners is 45x. The ladder in section 4 moves average order value from about $40 to about $53-56 (a 35-40 percent change). So: spend the effort on the path to an enquiry, then on price.

Scenario for the price changes alone (mix and attach rates are guesses; hours from section 3):
| Case | Avg order value | Hours per order | Net $/h after 10% fees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today, mix 60/35/5 | $39.5 | 6.1 | $5.9 |
| Today's prices, mix 40/50/10 | $46 | 7.9 | $5.2 |
| Proposed prices, mix 40/50/10, add-ons on 25% of orders at $20 | $56 | 7.0 | $7.2 |
To net $1,000 gross a month: about 25 orders at today's average, about 18 at the proposed average.

## 6. Conversion actions (no price change needed)
1. **Job page to WhatsApp.** On each job page, a button "Get a CV for this role" that opens WhatsApp with the job title and company pre-filled. This sits at the moment of intent and is the cheapest way to turn board traffic into enquiries. Copy must not claim the CV suits the role or improves odds (site copy rules).
2. **Free quick CV check.** On the services page and in the WhatsApp greeting: "Send your CV and I will tell you the three things I would fix first." It starts conversations, shows skill, and leads to a package. Limit it (for example one per person, first 10 a week) so it does not eat the hours.
3. **Testimonials.** The page currently has only labelled fictional before/after examples. After each delivery, ask the client for a one-sentence quote and permission to publish it. The first five real quotes are worth more than any price tweak. Never write a testimonial yourself.
4. **Plan-page layout (Nick Kolenda, "The Psychology of Pricing Plans", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VKlATx6NQbI).** Show three plans, same height, close together, prices near the top of each card, plan names bold, billing period below the price, a thin line above the button, mostly white background, and visually distinguish the most expensive plan so the higher number is seen first (anchoring). The current page already has three plans of equal height; the changes are small.
5. **Charm prices.** $29, $49, $149 and $99 a month already end in 9. The evidence for charm pricing is mostly secondary and vendor blogs, so treat it as plausible and free, not proven.
6. **Cash and trust.** For Executive, offer to take payment in two parts (half at the start, half at day 15) if buyers hesitate. Do not add this until a buyer asks.

## 7. Evidence quality and what I did not verify
- The two earlier video reviews (Saraev, Erickson) and the new one (Kolenda) are creator opinion; Kolenda cites academic studies but I did not open them. They give frameworks to test, not proof.
- The compromise effect (people prefer the middle option) is supported by the original Simonson and Tversky work but I read it only through secondary pages today. Treat "the middle tier will win" as likely, not certain, and watch the real mix.
- Competitor prices are headline "from" prices from search excerpts, mostly for buyers in high-income countries. They are a ceiling and a reference, not a measure of what your readers will pay.
- Hours per package are estimates. The order of magnitude (Executive far heavier than the others) is robust; the exact numbers are not.
- I have no traffic, enquiry or buyer-country data. GoatCounter requires a login.
