# Yireh Careers: how we write

Written 24 Oct 2026 after KT's feedback that the first emails were too long and too abrasive: "you are talking to human beings, not machines". This guide replaces the legal-first habit with a reader-first one. Use it for every email, WhatsApp message, post and page.

## The idea in one line
Write the way a kind, capable person would say it across a table. Say what the reader needs, once, plainly. Keep the legal facts, but put each one where it matters, in one friendly sentence.

## Who is reading
Someone job-hunting, often stressed, often on a phone, often reading in a second language. They want to know three things: what is happening, what do I do next, can I trust you. Every message answers those first.

## Our tone (the four dials from Nielsen Norman Group)
| Dial | Where we sit | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| Formal / casual | Conversational | "Thanks, we've started." not "We acknowledge receipt of your documents." |
| Serious / funny | Mostly serious, a little warm | No jokes in service messages. A light touch is fine in posts. |
| Respectful / irreverent | Respectful | We never talk down to anyone. No scolding. |
| Matter-of-fact / enthusiastic | Calm and matter-of-fact, glad to help | At most one exclamation mark in a message. No hype. |

Words we are not: legalese, salesy, bossy, over-excited, robotic.

## Rules
1. **Read it aloud.** If you would not say it to a person's face, rewrite it. (Monzo's test.)
2. **Bottom line first.** Open with what happened or what you need. Reasons come after, if at all.
3. **One message, one job.** One next step per message. If you have two things to say, you probably have two messages.
4. **Short.** Short sentences, short paragraphs, plain words, active voice ("we've started", not "work has been commenced"). Emails: roughly 60 to 110 words. WhatsApp: a few lines.
5. **Say "you" and "we".** Use their first name when we have it, "Hi there" when we do not.
6. **Ask, do not order.** "Could you email your CV to..." beats "You must send...". Use "please" once, not in every line.
7. **Legal facts: once, where it matters, in one plain sentence.** Examples: the 90-day deletion goes in the "order complete" message; the accuracy check goes in the branding message; the eligibility tip goes with jobs. Do not repeat disclaimers in every message. Anything longer lives on a page we link to (privacy, services, verify).
8. **Explain why, briefly, when we ask for something unusual.** "We only take documents by email so they stay private and in one place" lands better than "Do not send documents on WhatsApp."
9. **Own mistakes simply.** "Sorry about that" once, then fix it. Do not apologise when nothing went wrong.
10. **Never say**: guaranteed, no work permit needed, perfect for [a background], the only job board. These are still site-wide copy rules.
11. **No emoji in emails.** In WhatsApp, at most one, only where it genuinely helps.
12. **Sign as the team.** "The Yireh Careers team". No personal name, face or founder line.

## Subject lines and first lines (email)
- Name the thing: "Your documents are ready", "We've started on your CV".
- Under about 45 characters where possible. No all caps, no "!!!", no "RE:" tricks.
- The preview text says something the subject does not.

## WhatsApp specifics
- Greeting: say hello, say who you are, ask what they need, set a real expectation for when you will reply.
- Do not ask for detail before saying hello.
- Be honest about delays and about hours; a concrete time beats "ASAP".
- Replies are quick replies: keep each one short enough to read without scrolling.

## What the research says, and how far to trust it
- **Tone shapes how people feel about a brand, but the effect is modest.** In Nielsen Norman Group's test (50 US respondents), tone differences moved friendliness and formality ratings by about half a point to one point on a five-point scale. They advise matching tone to the reader's emotional state and avoiding the extremes. [NN/g, four dimensions of tone](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/)
- **Clear beats clever.** Mailchimp's style guide puts plainspoken and genuine first and says it is "always more important to be clear than entertaining"; forced humour "can be worse than none at all". [Mailchimp voice and tone](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/)
- **Serious things do not need stiff language.** Monzo says formal language makes a company "sound cold and distant", asks writers to read drafts aloud, to put the reader first, and says "clarity and kindness" lead even in legal and operational messages. [Monzo writing principles](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice/)
- **Transactional emails: lead with the event, one next step, cut the rest.** Consistent across Mailchimp, Omnisend, Mailjet and others (vendor blogs, so treat as practitioner advice). [Mailchimp on transactional email](https://mailchimp.com/resources/transactional-email-examples/)
- **People skim.** Put the key point first; short paragraphs and lists help. [Australian Style Manual, plain language](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/style-manual-resources/quick-guides/quick-guide-plain-language)
- **Legal information can be layered.** The UK ICO endorses a short notice with the key points at the moment it matters, linking to the full detail. [ICO on privacy information](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/the-right-to-be-informed/what-methods-can-we-use-to-provide-privacy-information/)
- **WhatsApp greetings** (practitioner blogs, not Meta guidance): short and warm, name the business, offer help, give a concrete reply time, one emoji at most, avoid stacked exclamation marks. [Trengo examples](https://trengo.com/blog/whatsapp-business-greeting-message)

What is not established: how our own readers respond. Judge this copy by replies, enquiries and what people say, and revise.

## Compliance still applies (what stayed, where it lives now)
- Delivery timing starts at onboarding: one sentence in the order and onboarding messages.
- Waiver of the cooling-off period by sending documents: one sentence in the payment-received message.
- Deletion within 90 days (30 days for a free check): in the completion and free-check messages.
- Refund rules: in the refund reply and on the services page.
- Fees, exchange rate and wrong-network warnings: in the payment messages, in plain words.
- Eligibility tip and "we do not promise interviews or offers": on the job messages and the services page, not in every email.
- A lawyer or accountant should still review terms before the company is registered; this guide only changes how we say things, not what we promise.
