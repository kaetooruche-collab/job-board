# Everything a founder should consider when picking a business name

Written 24 Oct 2026 for KT, for the job board plus career services brand. Each item says why it matters, how to check it, and where our project stands. "Done" means Claude ran it; "Open" means someone still has to. Costs and legal specifics change: confirm current fees and rules before relying on any figure.

## 1. Strategy: what the name must do
| Item | Why it matters | How to check | Our status |
|---|---|---|---|
| One brand or several | Every extra brand splits trust, reviews and search ranking. Buyers arrive from the board and pay the services: the receipt must say the name they saw. | Trace one buyer: page, checkout, receipt, bank statement, email. All must show the same name. | Decision: one brand (KT). |
| Who it is for | Worldwide job seekers, every level and field, many non-native English readers. | Test the name on 5-10 people from the target audience. | Open: not tested on people. |
| The story in one sentence | The name should support "verified remote jobs, no fees, real help with your CV". | Say it aloud in a sentence with the name. | Recommended name Farhaven: "a safe place, far away". |
| Name type | Strength of legal protection runs: generic < descriptive < suggestive < arbitrary < invented. Descriptive names ("CareerBridge", "HireReady") are hard to register and defend and are lost in search. | Ask: does the name simply describe the service? If yes, it is weak. | Farhaven is suggestive. |
| Room to grow | The brand may add employers, courses, a seeker plan. Avoid names that fence you in ("CV...", "...Jobs"). | List three things the business might sell in five years. | A neutral name chosen on purpose; domain variants carry "jobs" and "careers". |
| Tone | Polished, calm, reliable for stressed people, not hype. | Read the name and the tagline aloud to a stranger. | Open. |
| Length and shape | Short names are easier to type, say and remember. Under 10 letters, two or three syllables, no hyphens, numbers or odd spellings. | Count letters; dictate it over the phone and see how people spell it. | Open: do the phone test. |
| Not tied to place or person | Our rule (CLAUDE.md): no country or city. KT: not a personal name. | Read it for hidden place or person references. | Met by the shortlist. |

## 2. Trademark and legal clearance
| Item | Why it matters | How to check | Our status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registered marks, per country | A registered mark in your field can force a rename after you have built a brand. A trademark is territorial: the US, EU, UK, Turkey, Cyprus and so on are separate. | US: USPTO trademark search (Claude ran it for live exact and similar marks). EU: EUIPO / TMview. UK: UK IPO. International: WIPO Global Brand Database. Home country registry. | US screen done for 100+ names. EU, UK, WIPO, Cyprus, Turkey: **Open** (those sites block automated searches; KT or an attorney runs them). |
| Right classes | Marks are searched by class of goods and services. For us: 35 (employment, recruitment, placement, résumé and CV writing), 41 (career coaching, courses, training) and 42 (online platform and software). Each office's wording controls; check its own ID list. | Nice Classification alphabetical list; the USPTO ID Manual. | Screened classes 35, 41, 42, 9, 45, 36. |
| Similar-looking and sounding marks | Refusals are for "likelihood of confusion", not only identical names (Farhaven vs Fairhaven). | Search the name, phonetic twins, spelling variants and the name plus "Group", "Jobs", "Careers". | US fuzzy screen done; others Open. |
| Unregistered (common-law) use | A business using a name without registering still has rights. "Truly Remote" has no US registration but is a live competing job board. | Web search, app stores, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, domain history, Google Maps. | Web screen done (weak, US-only); deeper search Open. |
| Company names | A company register can hold the same or a near name. | Companies House (UK), OpenCorporates, the registry where KAYZ Global Ltd is registered. | Open. |
| Trade-name registration | A limited company that trades under another name often must register the trading name. | Ask the accountant or registered agent where KAYZ Global Ltd is registered. | Open. |
| Filing the trademark | Registration gives enforceable rights and a basis for marketplace and domain disputes. Takes months; costs vary by country; often needs a local attorney. Madrid Protocol can extend one filing to many countries. | Attorney quote; check each office's current fees. | Open: not before the name is chosen. |
| Copyright and logo | A name alone is not copyrighted; a logo or slogan can be. Any logo made with a tool or by a contractor needs a written assignment or clear licence. | Contract or tool licence for the logo; keep proof of authorship. | Open when the logo is made. |
| Disputes and watch | Squatters and look-alikes appear after launch. | Trademark watch service or a recurring manual search; set alerts. | Open. |
| Regulated words | Words like "recruitment agency", "bank", "university", "official" may need licences or are restricted. "Employment agency" is licensed in some countries. | Check where we operate; keep "agency" out of the name unless licensed. | Name has none; check the wording of the service description. |

## 3. Domains and email
| Item | Why it matters | How to check | Our status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact `.com` | Most trusted and the usual choice for email. Almost every meaningful `.com` is taken. | Registrar search (Claude used the Vercel registry lookup). | Not available for Farhaven (see decision note); alternatives priced. |
| Alternatives | `<name>jobs.com`, `get<name>.com`, `<name>hq.com`, `.work`, `.careers`, `.co`. Pick one primary and redirect the others. | Registrar; read renewal prices (first-year prices can be low and renewals high, e.g. `.careers`). | Priced for Farhaven (about $11 each for `.com` variants). |
| Who owns the taken ones | Parked, for sale, or an active business in your field. | Open the site; public registry record (RDAP/WHOIS) for age and expiry. | Done for Farfield, Farreach, Plumbline. |
| Typos and look-alikes | Scammers copy brands; typos lose visitors. | Register one or two likely typos and the plural or "get" form. | Open. |
| Registrar security | Losing a domain loses the business. | Two-factor login, registrar lock, auto-renew, correct billing email, long first term. | Open at purchase. |
| Domain privacy | Public registration data exposes the owner's name and address. | Enable WHOIS privacy; use a business contact. | Open at purchase. |
| Business email | A branded address is more trusted and lets newsletters pass spam filters. Mail providers expect authenticated senders: SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Brevo requires domain authentication for campaigns; sending from a free Gmail address performs poorly. | Set up a mail host (e.g. Google Workspace or another provider; check current prices), add SPF, DKIM, DMARC records, test with a mail-checker. | Today: yirehcareers@gmail.com (placeholder, can change). Open. |
| Mailbox plan | Separate addresses for support, privacy and billing avoid mixing. | Aliases: hello@, support@, privacy@, billing@. | Open. |
| Transition | Every old address and link in WhatsApp posts and emails must keep working. | Redirect the old site; forward the old inbox for a year; announce the change. | Open: the old Vercel link and Gmail address are in many places. |
| Hosting link | The name must map to the Vercel project domain. | Add the domain in Vercel; verify DNS. | Open. |

## 5. Language, culture and sound (global audience)
| Item | Why it matters | How to check | Our status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meaning in other languages | A fine English word can be rude or silly elsewhere. | Native speakers or a translator for Turkish, Arabic, Spanish, Portuguese, French, Hindi, Russian, Chinese, Indonesian, German, Swahili. | **Open**: no known problem for Farhaven, none verified. |
| Pronunciation | Names with "th", "wh", silent letters or "rr" clusters are hard for many learners. | Ask non-native speakers to say and spell it. | Open. |
| Spelling by ear | If people mis-type it, they land elsewhere. | Dictate the name; count the spellings. | Open. |
| Scripts | Right-to-left or non-Latin readers may transliterate the name. | Write it in Arabic or Cyrillic script and see whether it stays readable. | Open. |
| Cultural associations | Religious, political, historic or brand associations. | Search "<name> meaning"; image search. | Farhaven: "haven" is warm; "tax haven" is a possible association. |
| Initials and shortened forms | Nicknames and acronyms can be awkward. | Check the first letters and common shortening. | Open. |

## 6. Customers and market resonance
| Item | Why it matters | How to check | Our status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Five-second test | Does a stranger guess what the business does from the name plus a tagline? | Show name and tagline to 5-10 people for five seconds. | Open. |
| Trust cues | Job seekers fear scams; the name should feel stable, not flashy or "too good". | Ask: which name would you trust with your CV? | Open. |
| Distinct from competitors | Names close to rivals dilute you and invite confusion. | List 15 competitors; look for shared words (remote, career, hire, work). | Done for the main remote boards and CV services in the decision note. |
| Search results | Can you rank for your own name? A common word or another firm's name shares or steals results. | Search the name in quotes, then name + jobs, careers, resume. | Done roughly (US-only). Farfield rejected on this. |
| Customer language | The words readers use: "verified", "remote", "CV", "resume". | Reuse their words in the tagline, not the name. | Tagline "Every listing verified. Every link real." |

## 7. Business operations tied to the name
| Item | Why it matters | Our status |
|---|---|---|
| Legal entity name vs brand name | Contracts, invoices, tax and payment accounts use the legal name; brand and entity must be linked on the site. | KAYZ Global Ltd, trading as the brand: needs the registrar's confirmation. |
| Payment descriptor and receipts | The name on the bank statement should match the brand so buyers do not dispute charges. | Whop business name and descriptor to be set at production setup. |
| Terms, privacy and refund pages | They name the business; all must change at the rename. | Listed in the decision note. |
| Email templates and signatures | Existing Brevo templates cannot be edited, only recreated. | Open at rename. |
| Documents and files | PDF footers, WhatsApp greeting, invoices. | Open. |
| Cost | Domains, trademark search and filing, brand design, mail hosting, a lawyer's hour. | Domains about $45 for year one; the rest to be quoted. |

## 9. Decision method (how to finish)
1. Shortlist 3 names that pass sections 1, 3 and a US-level section 2 check (done: Farhaven, Farreach, Farfield).
2. Run the open checks for the shortlist: EUIPO/UK/WIPO/home registry, handles, language check, five-second test.
3. Pick one; buy the domains and handles the same day (so a squatter cannot react); file the trademark within the priority window an attorney advises.
4. Announce the change, migrate emails and links, keep redirects live for at least a year.
5. Record the decision and the evidence (this file and `drafts/decisions/2026-10-24-brand-name-and-founder-visibility.md`).

## Warnings that apply to any name
- Do not register the name as a company or domain before the trademark search; a company registration is not a trademark.
- A free domain does not mean a free name. A taken `.com` with no website often means a holder who will ask a high price or has plans.
- Do not announce the name before the handles and domains are secured.
- Do not rename after launch unless a legal threat forces it: every receipt, link and review carries the old name.
